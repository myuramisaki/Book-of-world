THE WASTELAND — YEAR 17 OF THE INVASION

The sky is the color of bruised iron. Above, their ships drift like poisoned clouds. They came seventeen years ago — from nowhere, from beyond the stars. They did not negotiate. They did not speak. They simply took. Cities burn in the distance. The air tastes of ash and fear.

You haven't eaten in two days. The streets are ruins. Most people are gone — taken, hiding, or worse. You move through what was once a library, now a hollow shell of concrete and memory. Something catches your eye beneath a fallen beam.

It is a book. Not like the others. Its leather cover pulses faintly, as if it breathes. No title. No author. Just a strange symbol etched into the front — three triangles woven together.

You reach out. Your fingers brush the cover. It is warm. Too warm. The pages open on their own. The words inside shift and change, rearranging themselves into a single sentence:

"YOU SEEK TO SAVE WHAT IS BROKEN? THEN ENTER. BUT KNOW THIS — THE DOOR SWINGS BOTH WAYS."

THE IN-BETWEEN

You open the book. The words swirl like smoke. The world tilts. The ruins vanish. The silence screams. You are falling — not through air, but through pages. Through time itself. The book pulls you in, consuming you whole.

When you land, you are not hurt. You stand in a vast, endless library. Shelves stretch upward into infinite darkness. A voice echoes from everywhere and nowhere — ancient, weary, powerful.

"THEY HAVE BROKEN THE WORLD FROM THE OUTSIDE. ONLY FROM THE INSIDE CAN IT BE MADE WHOLE AGAIN. FIVE CHAPTERS. FIVE TRIALS. FIVE FRAGMENTS OF THE KEY. COMPLETE THE BOOK, AND YOU SAVE YOUR WORLD. FAIL... AND YOUR WORLD BECOMES JUST ANOTHER STORY THAT ENDS."

Before you float five glowing doorways. Each leads to a trial. Each holds a fragment. Choose wisely — the order matters less than the truth within you.

CHAPTER V: THE FINAL CHOICE — THE CORE

All four fragments glow within your hands. They form a key. But before the final door stands the voice again — louder now, shaking the very foundations of the library.

"YOU HOLD THE POWER TO REWRITE REALITY. THE DOOR OPENS TWO WAYS. CHOOSE WISELY, FOR THIS DECISION WILL CHANGE EVERYTHING FOREVER."

Through one door, you can send the invaders away. But billions will have died already. The world will be broken, and slowly, painfully, humanity will rebuild.

Through the other door, you can turn back time itself. The invasion never happened. No one died. But you — you will remember everything. The pain. The loss. The faces of those who perished in a timeline that never was. You alone will carry the weight of what humanity was spared.

"SAVE THE FUTURE, OR HEAL THE PAST? WHICH BURDEN DO YOU CHOOSE TO CARRY?"

THE LONG REBUILDING

Above the ruins of Earth, the alien ships flicker — and then they are gone. Not destroyed. Just... returned. Sent back across the void from which they came.

You stand once more in the ruined library. The book lies closed at your feet. Survivors emerge from cellars and tunnels. They do not know what happened. They only know the terror above has lifted. The world is scarred. Cities must be rebuilt. Graves must be honored. It will take generations. But humanity is free.

You do not tell anyone what you did. You pick up a shovel instead. There is work to be done. The book vanishes when you glance back — its purpose fulfilled, its guardians at peace.

"Heroism isn't about fixing the past. It's about giving the future a chance to grow."

THE SILENT BURDEN

You turn the key. Reality ripples like water. The library dissolves around you. You blink — and you are standing in the library ruins, but the ruins are gone. It is seventeen years ago. The sky is blue. The sun shines. The invasion fleet is still light-years away, diverted by forces no one will ever understand.

You are standing on a sidewalk in a city that will never fall. People walk by, laughing, talking, living lives that would have been cut short. They are alive. Everyone is alive. But you remember the ash. The screams. The empty skies. You carry a history that exists only in your mind.

You never speak of it. You live your life, love your friends, cherish every moment. Sometimes at night you wake up sweating, gasping from dreams of burning skies. But then you look out the window and see the stars — and you know it was worth it. A silent guardian. A memory of what never was, but always could have been.

"The greatest sacrifice is not one's life, but one's peace — so that others may never know war."
